# Churn Is Dead authority and growth operating plan

**Operating window:** 25 August to 20 October 2026  
**Primary metric:** qualified actions, not raw reach. A qualified action is a subscription, playbook open, reader-pulse response, meaningful reply, or CS Analyzer demo visit attributable to an issue or campaign.

## Brand position

Churn Is Dead is the evidence-led operating system for enterprise Customer Success.

The weekly promise is deliberately narrow:

1. One defensible argument.
2. One operating model.
3. One tool the reader can run.
4. One decision the reader can make differently.

The website remains canonical. LinkedIn is the primary discovery channel. Medium is a selective evergreen syndication channel after the canonical article is live. External copy always keeps its own approval record.

## Weekly operating rhythm

### Wednesday to Friday: collect inputs

- Review aggregate growth and activation signals.
- Read reader-pulse results and approved replies.
- Identify the costly decision, not merely the popular keyword.
- Check the previous eight issues for topic and emotional-register duplication.

### Sunday: build the evidence package

- Research primary and official sources first.
- Record every external claim in `evidence.json`.
- Keep Kuber's experience out unless the exact claim exists in `author-experience.md`.
- Draft the issue and a playbook that changes a real meeting, handoff, or decision.
- Validate the package. Leave approval pending.

### Before Tuesday: approval and staging

- Review title, thesis, sources, limits, CTA, playbook, and external adaptations.
- Record website approval separately from LinkedIn or Medium approval.
- Stage the exact package to Supabase only after deterministic validation passes.

### Tuesday: publish and learn

- Publish the canonical website issue at 08:00 UTC.
- Verify the article, playbook, canonical URL, social card, sitemap, RSS, and email prerequisites.
- Publish one approved LinkedIn adaptation with a campaign-specific UTM.
- Record any correction immediately and preserve a visible history.

## Distribution experiments

Run one clean variable at a time.

| Window | Test | Keep constant | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Establish complete site and welcome-email baseline | Tuesday issue structure and CTA | Do not declare a winner |
| Weeks 3–4 | Hard-problem LinkedIn opening versus operating-model opening | Canonical article and landing CTA | Keep the opening that produces qualified site actions, not only impressions |
| Weeks 5–6 | One LinkedIn Newsletter adaptation and one evergreen Medium import | Canonical URL and topic | Continue only if each produces attributable subscribers or tool use |
| Weeks 7–8 | Repeat the best channel-format combination | Editorial quality and publication cadence | Remove formats that create reach without qualified action |

Medium imports must use Medium's import flow or an explicit canonical URL. LinkedIn Newsletter is a monthly test, not a second weekly publishing obligation.

## Monthly brand review

Ask five questions:

1. Which topic produced the most qualified action per measured visit?
2. Which playbook was opened and then revisited?
3. Which issue earned substantive replies rather than generic reactions?
4. Which factual claim, example, or headline created avoidable trust risk?
5. What should be stopped because it adds production volume without reader value?

The brand does not need more content for its own sake. It needs a body of work that becomes more useful, more trustworthy, and easier to navigate every week.
