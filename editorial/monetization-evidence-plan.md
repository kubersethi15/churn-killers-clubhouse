# Churn Is Dead monetization evidence plan

**Status:** research only. No paid offer is approved for launch.

Monetization must strengthen the promise that made the audience subscribe: one defensible argument, one operating model, one usable tool, and one clearer decision. It must not turn Churn Is Dead into an undifferentiated course catalogue, generic community, or lead-generation wrapper around the newsletter.

## Current evidence baseline

Read on 24 August 2026, without exposing identities:

- Supabase table statistics estimate 325 subscriber records and 151 first-party growth events.
- The CS Analyzer waitlist and feedback tables each estimate zero records.
- Google Search Console reported 18 clicks from 246 impressions in the preceding three months, with 16 pages indexed.
- Complete campaign-variant attribution and a safe email feedback loop are not yet deployed.

This proves that an owned audience exists. It does not prove that readers will pay, which problem they will pay to solve, or whether software, facilitation, or advisory work is the right delivery model.

## Commercial principles

1. Validate a costly decision before designing an offer.
2. Sell the implementation of an operating mechanism, not access to information already published for free.
3. Keep the newsletter and core playbooks useful without purchase.
4. Do not infer willingness to pay from page views, likes, downloads, or subscriber count.
5. Use aggregate behavioural signals for prioritisation. Collect identifiable research notes only with explicit participant consent and never commit them to the repository.
6. Run one commercial learning step at a time, after the current Tuesday acquisition baseline is complete.
7. Require Kuber's explicit approval before naming a price, publishing an offer page, inviting buyers, accepting payment, or making a delivery promise.

## Offer ladder: hypotheses, not products

| Order | Hypothesis | Why it fits the brand | Evidence required before testing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Facilitated operating review | Applies one existing Churn Is Dead tool to a real team decision without pretending the PDF alone creates change | Repeated demand around the same decision plus opt-in conversations confirming a current owner, costly workaround, and near-term decision |
| 2 | Short operating-system sprint | Converts the review into an implemented cadence, decision record, and ownership model | A facilitated review produces a useful decision and the buyer asks for implementation help |
| 3 | CS Analyzer team workflow | Makes evidence extraction and decision preparation repeatable | Sustained demo use, substantive feedback, repeat usage, and a clear team workflow that software improves beyond a manual template |
| 4 | Select advisory engagement | Supports a senior leader through a high-consequence CS operating redesign | Multiple qualified requests that cannot be served by the standard review or sprint |
| 5 | Sponsorship or partner underwriting | Could fund research while leaving reader access free | Stable qualified reach, a written independence policy, and a partner whose commercial interest does not determine editorial conclusions |

Start with facilitated work if the evidence supports it. It tests willingness to pay and delivery value with less product speculation than building a paid software tier. Do not launch a workshop merely because it is first in the table.

## Evidence gates

The counts below are internal decision rules, not market benchmarks.

### Gate 0: measurement is trustworthy

- Complete the first seven-day Tuesday distribution baseline.
- Deploy aggregate campaign acquisition and current-active reporting.
- Keep email disabled until the signed webhook, suppression handling, and provider-health checks pass.

**Decision:** no commercial research request while this gate is incomplete.

### Gate 1: one problem repeats

A problem may advance only when it appears across at least two independent aggregate signals, such as reader-pulse responses, repeated playbook opens, issue visits, substantive approved replies, or CS Analyzer demo behaviour. One high-performing post is insufficient.

**Decision:** select one problem for research; do not present an offer.

### Gate 2: buyers describe a costly decision

Run a small set of opt-in research conversations only after approval. Before advancing, at least three qualified participants must independently describe:

- the same decision or workflow;
- what happens if it remains unresolved;
- who owns the decision and budget;
- the current workaround and its cost in time, risk, or missed commercial action;
- a credible timeframe for changing it.

Do not count compliments, general interest, or requests for more free content as buying evidence.

**Decision:** write one offer hypothesis with no public page and no payment collection.

### Gate 3: willingness to pay is explicit

Present the proposed outcome, scope, delivery mechanism, and exclusions in private research. Ask participants what they would need to approve budget and what alternatives they would compare. Record price reactions as research; do not manufacture a discount or scarcity deadline.

**Decision:** recommend a pilot price and capacity only from observed buyer language. Kuber must approve both.

### Gate 4: one manual pilot

Run one paid pilot before building a scaled product. Define the decision, inputs, deliverables, participant responsibilities, confidentiality, and success evidence in writing. Do not promise churn reduction, NRR improvement, or other outcomes the engagement cannot control.

**Decision:** continue only if the buyer received a useful decision mechanism, the delivery effort is sustainable, and there is evidence of repeat or referral demand.

## Measurement

Keep commercial reporting aggregate and separate from editorial approval.

| Stage | Primary evidence | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Problem discovery | Qualified responses by problem | No identity or free text in growth-event analytics |
| Research | Participants confirming owner, consequence, workaround, and timing | Explicit consent; private notes outside the repository |
| Pilot | Proposal acceptance, completion, and buyer-confirmed usefulness | No fabricated ROI or testimonials |
| Repeatability | Repeat request, referral, delivery hours, and gross contribution | Stop if custom work cannot become a coherent mechanism |
| Productisation | Repeat workflow usage and team-level demand | Do not use model novelty as the value proposition |

Revenue is an outcome metric after launch, not evidence that the offer should exist before launch.

## First research candidate

The first candidate should be selected after the distribution baseline, not fixed today. Based on the current editorial portfolio, the shortlist is:

- Product Friction Review: help CS and Product turn recurring customer friction into an explicit decision record.
- Renewal Evidence Review: test whether a renewal forecast contains decision-quality evidence and named ownership.
- CS Decision Rights Review: redesign where AI prepares evidence and where accountable human judgment remains.

Choose the candidate with repeated qualified behaviour, not the most provocative headline.
