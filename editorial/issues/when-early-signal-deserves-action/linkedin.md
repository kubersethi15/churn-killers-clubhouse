An early warning signal does not deserve action merely because it arrived early.

Usage falls.

A sponsor cancels twice.

A milestone slips.

Each could reveal customer risk.

Each could also be normal variation, weak data or a plan that was wrong from the start.

That is why a health score and an intervention trigger are different tools.

A score summarises the account.

A trigger specifies:

The customer consequence.

The exact signal and source.

The relevant baseline.

The threshold, window and persistence.

The verification step.

The response owner and deadline.

The reset condition.

If a signal cannot point to a different action, it may belong on a dashboard rather than in an intervention queue.

Do not rebuild the entire health model first.

Choose one recurring risk. Write one rule. Test it against three timelines. Then run it prospectively while the aggregate score stays unchanged.
