## Reddit (r/CustomerSuccess)

**Title:** Does renaming CSMs to "Forward Deployed Engineers" actually fix anything, or just move the problem?

There's a story going around from a recent conference. An AI company couldn't fill a "head of customer success" role. Two candidates in over two months. They changed the title to "forward deployed engineer" and the pipeline filled overnight.

Everyone's using it as proof that "customer success" is a dead term. And for hiring, sure, the evidence is right there.

But something bugs me about it. The title change fixed the candidate side. The customer side didn't change at all. And the customer is who decides whether you renew.

My theory is that buyers didn't go defensive on "customer success" because of the word. They went defensive because the role quietly became a commercial one. CS reports to the CRO now. NRR is the board number. Comp is tied to expansion. So the "advocate inside the vendor" is actually being paid to sell you more, and buyers picked up on it after enough check-ins that drifted to seat counts.

If that's true, renaming to FDE just buys time until the customer learns the new title has a quota attached too.

Curious what people who've actually done a rebrand have seen. Did the trust problem follow the role to the new name, or did the technical framing genuinely reset the dynamic? I got into this in my newsletter this week but mostly want to hear real experiences.
