The discourse keeps getting this wrong.

Everyone treats "customer success" like a word that aged badly. Like calling someone a "webmaster" in 2026.

It isn't that.

The title was accurate. Then somewhere between 2019 and now, the job changed underneath the word.

CS started reporting to the CRO. NRR became the only board-level number. Variable comp got bolted onto expansion.

The role sold to customers as "your advocate inside the vendor" was now measured, promoted, and paid on how much more it could extract from them.

Buyers figured this out. Not because they read your comp plan.

Because they sat across from enough CSMs whose "strategic check-in" always found its way to a seat count by minute forty.

They learned the person introduced as their partner had a quota with their logo on it.

That's what "customer success" signals to a sharp technical buyer now. Not incompetence. Not fluff.

A disguised commercial motion pointed at them.

Here's my question. If you rename the role, what happens the moment your customer learns what the new word is incentivised to do?

Renaming your CSMs won't pay down the credibility debt. But most leaders skip straight to the title change anyway.

Here's the audit I'd run first. Map each customer-facing role to what the customer actually experiences.

1. What were they PROMISED
What did sales say this role would do for them?

2. What they're MEASURED on
Pull the comp plan. Is it retention, expansion, or a hidden quota?

3. What the customer EXPERIENCES
Where does the "strategic check-in" actually land by minute forty?

4. The GAP
The distance between promise and experience is your credibility debt. That's the real problem.

5. The FIX order
Build the deliverable first. Touch the comp plan second. Rename the role last, if at all.

Most orgs do this in reverse. They rename first and hope the deliverable shows up later.

That's refinancing debt at a worse rate.

The honest version is harder, slower, and more expensive than the rename. It requires you to build something, not borrow a word.

But the customer keeps the ledger. And the customer collects at renewal.
