## Reddit (r/CustomerSuccess)

**Title:** Does anyone else think 'full coverage' has become a meaningless CS metric?

Something's been bugging me about how we report on digital CS.

A lot of teams proudly hit "100% account coverage" after standing up automation on the long tail. Nurture emails, in-app nudges, chatbots, renewal reminders. Coverage report goes from 60% to 100% and it looks like a win.

But coverage only measures whether a machine touched the account. It doesn't measure whether a human ever had a real conversation with the customer. And in my experience those are wildly different things.

The failure mode I keep seeing: the champion who understood why they bought leaves in month five. The new internal owner inherited the tool and never wanted it. Daily users keep logging in out of habit so the health score stays green. Sixty days before renewal an automated reminder lands in an inbox nobody is defending. The account quietly doesn't renew.

That's not a decision to churn. That's silence.

What gets me is the NRR math. A long-tail segment printing 97% (below breakeven) hides perfectly behind an enterprise segment at 118%. Blended, everything looks fine.

The one thing I've started measuring: days between an account's last real human conversation and its renewal date. That gap is basically your blind spot.

Curious whether others separate automation touches from actual conversations in reporting, or if that's just not tracked anywhere. I dug into this more in my newsletter this week if useful, but mostly want to hear how other teams handle it.
