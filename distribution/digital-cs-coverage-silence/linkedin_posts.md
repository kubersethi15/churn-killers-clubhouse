Most CS teams track one number: how many accounts got touched.

Wrong number.

Split it into two columns. Call it the Silence Ledger.

Column one: accounts an automation touched. Emails sent. Nudges fired. Chatbot sessions logged.

Column two: accounts a human actually spoke with. A call. A real conversation. A person who knows the champion's name.

Here is the uncomfortable part. Only column two predicts renewal in the long tail.

Column one is theater. It makes the dashboard glow green. It lets you report 100% coverage. It gives you a story for the board.

But a customer whose champion left, whose new owner never wanted the tool, whose usage is coasting on habit, will not be saved by a re-engagement email. That account is silent. And silence is not the same as safe.

When you look at your own book, most of what you call coverage lives in column one.

So here is the question I keep asking CS leaders and rarely get a clean answer to.

What percentage of your "covered" accounts have had a real human conversation in the last 90 days?

"We cover 100% of accounts" is the most dangerous sentence in a QBR.

Here is how to test whether it's a capability you built or a silence you bought.

1. Split coverage into two columns. Accounts an automation touched vs accounts a human actually spoke with. Only measure the second one going forward.

2. Find the blackout window. In your lowest-ARR segment, measure the days between the last human conversation and the renewal date. That gap is your undetected churn window.

3. Unbundle blended NRR. Report the long tail separately. A 97 hiding behind an enterprise 118 is a fire behind a wall.

4. Run the cost-to-silence sum. Headcount you saved on the tier vs the GRR you lost. If it's negative, you have a case to redeploy, not a saving to defend.

5. Audit involuntary churn. Check what share of that tier's churn is failed payments, not decisions. A blacked-out account that just needs a new card is losing you money for no reason.

Most teams do zero of these. They report the coverage number and move on.
