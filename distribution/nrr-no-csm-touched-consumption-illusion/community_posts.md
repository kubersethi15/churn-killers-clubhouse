## Reddit (r/CustomerSuccess)

**Title:** In consumption pricing, how do you tell which retention your CS team actually caused?

Something has been bugging me about how we measure CS impact under consumption or usage-based pricing.

Snowflake reports around 125% net revenue retention. Datadog around 120. The industry treats those as proof of world-class customer success. But realistically, no QBR moved those numbers. Customers consume more compute every quarter because their own data volume grows. The pricing model is generating most of that retention, not the account team.

If that is true at the top, it is true in most consumption books. I recently decomposed an account that grew 118%. Turned out most of the growth came from a single data migration the customer ran in the spring. Every record passed through the meter as ingest. Nobody on the CS side planned it or even knew until the invoice moved. We were about to book it as a win.

The scarier flip side is that the meter reports churn last. Usage velocity can be collapsing while the dollar number still looks healthy, so a green account can already be dying months before renewal.

How are you all separating earned retention from metered retention in practice? Are you tracking usage velocity as a leading indicator instead of dollars? Genuinely curious what works. I ended up writing a full breakdown of this in my Churn Is Dead newsletter, but mostly want to hear how others handle it.
