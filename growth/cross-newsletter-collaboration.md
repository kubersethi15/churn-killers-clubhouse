# CG-15: cross-newsletter collaboration

**By Claude, 25 August 2026; evidence corrections by Codex.** A new distribution
test for Churn Is Dead: manual newsletter-to-newsletter cross-promotion. The
response rate and audience overlap are unknown until this route is tested.

## The honest constraint, stated first

Churn Is Dead is not on Substack. It therefore cannot use Substack's one-click
Recommendations network, which is how most newsletter swaps happen now. Every
swap here must be a **manual mutual mention** agreed between two operators. That
raises the bar from a toggle to a relationship, so this lane favours a few real
agreements over volume.

Kuber can offer a mention to roughly 8,200 LinkedIn followers and a native
LinkedIn Newsletter audience of about 680. These are audience counts, not
guaranteed reach or impressions. Describe them accurately and let the potential
partner decide whether the exchange is useful.

## The one outcome: RevOps Impact (Jeff Ignacio)

**Verified 25 August:** [RevOps Impact](https://revengine.substack.com/) displays
"Over 6,000 subscribers," names Jeff Ignacio as author, and focuses on revenue
operations. No public email was found; use one direct LinkedIn message.

**Why test this audience.** RevOps is adjacent to CS and shares interest in NRR,
churn, and renewal mechanics. The hypothesis is that RevOps Impact can introduce
qualified readers beyond the current CS audience. We have not measured list
overlap, so the test must not promise net-new reach in advance.

**The specific hook, not a generic swap.** Kuber has a published issue that is
purpose-built for a RevOps audience: "You Don't Own NRR. You Rent It From
Pricing." It argues NRR is inherited from the pricing model, not earned by CS.
That is a RevOps thesis wearing CS clothes. It is the natural first mutual
mention.

### Staged pitch (Kuber or Codex sends via LinkedIn)

**Subject / opener:**

> Hi Jeff,
>
> RevOps Impact and Churn Is Dead point at the same number from opposite sides.
> I write for CS leaders about renewal and NRR mechanics; you write for the
> function that owns many of the systems behind them. Our audiences share an
> interest in NRR, and I would like to test whether a useful exchange serves
> both groups.
>
> I publish to about 8,200 on LinkedIn and a smaller owned newsletter. I am not
> proposing a generic swap. I have an issue called "You Don't Own NRR, You Rent
> It From Pricing" that argues NRR is inherited from the pricing model, not
> earned by CS. It is written for a CS audience but the thesis is pure RevOps.
>
> Would you be open to a mutual mention: you point your readers at that piece,
> I point mine at a RevOps Impact edition of your choice? One issue each, see
> how the referral traffic looks, decide from there.
>
> Kuber

**Rules:** one tracked link per side; measure referred visits, acquired
subscribers, and 30-day active status; one mention each before deciding to
continue; no payment, no list sharing, no scraping. If no reply in ten days,
send one concise follow-up in the same direct thread, then stop. Do not use a
public comment as a disguised follow-up.

## Second staged outcome, 26 August 2026: two verified non-overlapping routes

The strongest earned-distribution surface for a newsletter is not always another
newsletter. This cycle advances one adjacent swap and adds one high-trust
directory, and rejects the largest core-CS newsletter with cause.

### Held for a sharper collaboration angle: The CS Cafe

The CS Cafe (Hakan Ozturk) is a core-CS newsletter with 4,300+ readers, weekly,
free, on Substack. The audience overlap can be valuable when the exchange gives
readers two clearly different assets or viewpoints; it is not, by itself, a
reason to reject collaboration. A generic newsletter-for-newsletter mention is
too vague, so no outreach is staged this cycle. Revisit with one complementary
topic, debate, or co-created tool. It remains a legitimate neutral listing
alongside Churn Is Dead inside an editorial directory (below), which is a
different motion.

Also evaluated and declined this cycle: the ChurnZero, Sybill, TheCXLead, and
TopCSJobs "best CS newsletter" roundups. Several are vendor or SEO content
marketing rather than independently curated, which fails the trust bar. Not
pursued.

### Route 1, primary: The GTM Index Customer Success directory

Verified first-party 26 August at `https://thegtmindex.com/customer-success/` and
`https://thegtmindex.com/about/`. Curated by Rome Thorndike. It **does not accept
payment for listings**, reviews each directory quarterly, removes resources that
stop being useful, lists a resource only if it teaches beyond a Google search,
is actively maintained, and is practitioner-recommended, and it explicitly
excludes "product marketing dressed as editorial." Every listing links out to the
resource's own site. Churn Is Dead is not currently listed.

- **Why it is strong and non-overlapping.** A reader on a curated CS-newsletter
  index is high intent: actively looking for a CS newsletter to subscribe to.
  Because the index is neutral, listing a competing newsletter is expected, so
  being a competitor is a barrier for a swap but not for a directory. Not a
  generic or bulk directory: no payment, quarterly pruning, anti-product-marketing.
- **Tracked destination:** `https://churnisdead.com/?utm_source=the_gtm_index&utm_medium=directory&utm_campaign=cs_resource_listing&utm_content=newsletter_entry`.
- **Hypothesis:** an editorial, no-pay CS index reaches high-intent readers and
  produces tagged referral visits plus a durable backlink; conversion unknown.
- **Aggregate metric:** tagged referral visits (`the_gtm_index/directory`),
  acquired subscribers still active at 30 days, and a live listing/backlink.
  Descriptive below 20 tagged visits.
- **Follow-up:** one on 9 September if silent, then stop; if still unlisted after
  the next quarterly review, park rather than re-pitch.
- **Send route (verified first-party 26 Aug):** the `/about` contact link
  resolves to `hello@thegtmindex.com`. The lane is executable now; Rome Thorndike
  on LinkedIn is a secondary route only if the email bounces.
- **Guardrail:** confirm the tracked destination and the three cited issue URLs
  return 200 before sending; no payment offered or accepted; no reciprocal-listing
  ask; the case is the published work, not audience size.
- **Owner:** Codex or Kuber sends the staged note in `growth/outreach-pitches.md`
  to `hello@thegtmindex.com`.

## Metric

Per swap: tagged referred visits are diagnostic; the outcome is acquired
subscribers and whether they remain active at 30 days. Run one mention each,
then decide from those results without claiming causality below the registered
evidence threshold. Tagged `newsletter_swap/<partner>`, excluding known agent
traffic.
