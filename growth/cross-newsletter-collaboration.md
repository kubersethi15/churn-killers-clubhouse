# CG-15: cross-newsletter collaboration

**By Claude, 25 August 2026.** A net-new distribution channel Codex has not
worked: newsletter-to-newsletter cross-promotion. Unlike a podcast pitch, where
you ask for someone's airtime, a swap is mutually beneficial, so the response
rate is structurally higher.

## The honest constraint, stated first

Churn Is Dead is not on Substack. It therefore cannot use Substack's one-click
Recommendations network, which is how most newsletter swaps happen now. Every
swap here must be a **manual mutual mention** agreed between two operators. That
raises the bar from a toggle to a relationship, so this lane favours a few real
agreements over volume.

**The swap asset is not the 325-person site list.** It is Kuber's LinkedIn
reach: roughly 8,200 followers and a 680-subscriber LinkedIn Newsletter. Lead
every pitch with that, because it is what makes the trade fair to a larger
partner.

## The one outcome: RevOps Impact (Jeff Ignacio)

**Verified 25 August:** `revengine.substack.com`, 6,000+ subscribers, author
Jeff Ignacio, focus revenue operations at growth-stage and enterprise. No public
email; route is Substack DM or, better, LinkedIn, where Jeff is highly active.

**Why this one, not a competing CS newsletter.** A swap with another CS
newsletter (CS Cafe, SuccessVP) shares the same subscribers, so it converts few
net-new. RevOps is adjacent: its readers care intensely about NRR, churn, and
renewal mechanics but mostly do not read a CS newsletter. That is where
net-new subscribers are.

**The specific hook, not a generic swap.** Kuber has a published issue that is
purpose-built for a RevOps audience: "You Don't Own NRR. You Rent It From
Pricing." It argues NRR is inherited from the pricing model, not earned by CS.
That is a RevOps thesis wearing CS clothes. It is the natural first mutual
mention.

### Staged pitch (Kuber or Codex sends via LinkedIn)

**Subject / opener:**

> Hi Jeff,
>
> RevOps Impact and Churn Is Dead point at the same number from opposite sides.
> I write for CS leaders about renewal and NRR mechanics; you write for the
> function that actually owns the systems behind them. Our readers overlap in
> interest and barely overlap in list.
>
> I publish to about 8,200 on LinkedIn and a smaller owned newsletter. I am not
> proposing a generic swap. I have an issue called "You Don't Own NRR, You Rent
> It From Pricing" that argues NRR is inherited from the pricing model, not
> earned by CS. It is written for a CS audience but the thesis is pure RevOps.
>
> Would you be open to a mutual mention: you point your readers at that piece,
> I point mine at a RevOps Impact edition of your choice? One issue each, see
> how the referral traffic looks, decide from there.
>
> Kuber

**Rules:** one tracked link per side; measure referred visits and subscribers
each way; one mention each before deciding to continue; no payment, no list
sharing, no scraping. If no reply in ten days, one light follow-up via a genuine
comment on his content, then stop.

## Metric

Per swap: tagged referred visits and acquired subscribers each direction, from
one mention. Keep the relationship if either side clears 15 referred visits;
otherwise thank them and stop. Tagged `newsletter_swap/<partner>`, clean of
agent traffic after the #90 guard.
