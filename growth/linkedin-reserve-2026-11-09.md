### Exact draft caption

Your biggest customer doesn't automatically get the next engineer.

Here's a made-up case.

Their export is broken. There's a clumsy workaround. Renewal is close, and they're threatening to leave.

A smaller customer can't complete a core workflow. No workaround. Same support entitlement. Nobody has threatened anything.

One engineer is free.

I'd start with the blocked workflow and put a leader on the phone with the larger customer.

The renewal risk is real. So is the cost of leaving the other customer's work stopped.

If a cancellation threat changes the priority, I want us to say what we're putting behind it.

Who gets the engineer on your team?

### Exact document copy

1. **Your biggest customer is second in line.** A fictional priority call. The largest renewal this quarter has a broken export and an awkward workaround. A smaller customer has a stopped core workflow and no workaround. Both have the same support entitlement. One engineer can take either issue. Who goes first?
2. **There is a real case for the renewal.** The revenue matters. The workaround is costly. Losing trust could make the renewal harder. Those points deserve a leader's attention. They don't restore the other customer's stopped workflow. Neither issue involves security, data loss or an unmet emergency commitment; those facts would change the case.
3. **I'd restore the blocked workflow first.** I'd put a leader on the larger customer's call, explain the priority and agree the next update without inventing a fix time. If leadership chooses the renewal first, I'd want them to say which customer's work stays blocked. Who gets the engineer on your team?
