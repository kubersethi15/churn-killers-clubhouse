# Churn Is Dead growth operating system

**Start:** 24 August 2026

**Goal:** turn borrowed attention into attributable, retained newsletter subscribers and repeat use of Churn Is Dead tools.

Editorial quality is the product foundation. It is not the distribution strategy. Growth runs through four loops that can operate outside the weekly publishing workflow.

## North-star scorecard

The primary outcome is an acquired subscriber who is still active after 30 days. Report it by source and campaign. Do not optimise for followers, impressions, reactions, or raw sessions in isolation.

Track these in order:

1. Unique tagged visits.
2. Qualified action sessions: signup, playbook open, reader response, or CS Analyzer demo visit.
3. Acquired subscribers.
4. 30-day active subscribers by acquisition source.
5. Meaningful replies, invitations, and partner introductions.

LinkedIn profile views and follower growth are relationship indicators. They are not newsletter growth unless the site action is attributable.

## The four growth loops

### 1. Founder relationship loop

The social manager leaves three substantive, no-link comments per weekday on active posts from relevant CS operators already listed in the shared Engagement List.

- Add an operating insight, useful distinction, or real question.
- Do not pitch Churn Is Dead, paste a link, or use generic praise.
- Log the profile, post, and comment URLs in columns K:P of the Engagement List.
- Review replies, profile views, invitations, and eventual direct traffic weekly.

This is always-on relationship work, not a tagged acquisition experiment.

### 2. Borrowed-audience loop

Earn access to established CS audiences with one useful contribution at a time:

- contributed articles with a canonical link;
- guest podcast conversations around one defensible operating idea;
- community answers where the problem is already being discussed;
- a co-hosted teardown, office hour, or template session with a credible partner.

The contribution must stand on its own without a pitch. The author bio or final resource line carries one tagged canonical link.

### 3. Tool and referral loop

Every issue should create a reason to return or share:

- one decision-ready playbook;
- post-signup LinkedIn and copy-link referral actions;
- one reader response that shapes a future issue;
- the CS Analyzer as a deeper problem-solving path when it is genuinely relevant.

Do not add multiple competing calls to action to the same issue. A playbook can create the first useful action; the signup confirmation creates the referral action.

### 4. Compounding discovery loop

The website remains canonical. LinkedIn Newsletter and Medium are selective distribution surfaces, not duplicate weekly obligations.

- Test one LinkedIn Newsletter adaptation monthly after the first acquisition baseline.
- Import one strong evergreen issue to Medium only after the canonical article is live.
- Use the platform's canonical controls and one tagged link back to the original.
- Stop any surface that creates reach but no qualified actions in two measured tests.

## Experiment discipline

Only one tagged acquisition experiment may be active at a time. Relationship comments, research, and partner pitching may continue because they do not publish a competing tracked acquisition link.

The first baseline is the single Tuesday LinkedIn launch post from 25 August 08:15 UTC through 1 September 08:14 UTC. During that window:

- no Medium import;
- no second launch post;
- no LinkedIn Newsletter edition;
- no CTA or campaign-tag change;
- no community link drop.

If the baseline receives fewer than 20 unique tagged visits, report it as descriptive evidence. Do not infer a conversion winner from a tiny sample.

## Borrowed-audience sequence

1. Pitch one contributed article to Customer Success Collective.
2. Pitch the same operating territory as a podcast conversation only if the angle is distinct.
3. Answer one existing Gain Grow Retain or Customer Success Network discussion without a link; add a canonical resource only when the community rules and question make it useful.
4. Test a LinkedIn Newsletter adaptation.
5. Test one Medium canonical import.
6. Repeat only the channel that produces acquired subscribers or meaningful tool use.

## Monetisation readiness

Do not launch a paid offer merely because the site can take payment. Begin customer discovery when one problem repeatedly produces all three signals:

1. at least three separate issues or discussions attract qualified action;
2. at least ten readers independently describe the same costly problem or five agree to a discovery conversation;
3. a free playbook is repeatedly opened or used, not merely downloaded once.

Then test one manually delivered offer before building a course or membership:

1. a team operating-system workshop;
2. an evidence-led CS operating review;
3. a small design-partner cohort around the proven problem.

Sponsorship is a later option, after stable readership and a written separation between editorial conclusions and commercial influence. Paid ads, bought lists, gated community spam, and mass cold outreach are excluded.

## Weekly growth review

Every Monday:

1. close the prior experiment with aggregate evidence;
2. update the experiment register and channel pipeline;
3. keep, revise, or stop the tested motion;
4. activate at most one next tagged experiment;
5. choose one relationship or partner action that compounds even if no link is published.
