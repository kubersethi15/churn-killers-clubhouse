Take the health score off the screen at your next renewal review.

Then ask the CSM: “If your sponsor left tomorrow, who would defend this spend?”

Not who attends the QBR. Not who logs in the most. Who would explain to Finance what the company would lose by cutting the contract?

If you can’t name that person, the account can have great adoption and still be a bad bet for the forecast.

I’d ask for three things before calling it commit:
• A recent outcome the customer can describe in their own words.
• A named person who can defend the spend.
• One thing that would make us lower the forecast.

If the answer to the last one is “nothing,” the review isn’t testing the forecast.

What would make you take a green account out of commit?

## Thursday 2026-11-26 at17:30Sydney

Calendar row325. Creative:Codex / Kuber-side existing contribution. Job:Authority / sharp AI opinion. Asset:No asset; text only.

An AI agent that handles 80% of cases can still make your CX team slower.

Say a manual case takes 10 minutes. The agent clears 80 out of 100. A human spends 3 minutes checking each of those 80. The 20 exceptions take 40 minutes each because someone has to reconstruct what happened.

Before AI: 1,000 human minutes.
After AI: 1,040.

The 80% looks great in a deck. The team just lost 40 minutes.

Before calling a pilot efficient, run the same case twice. First, give the agent enough evidence to act. Then change one fact that should make it stop. See if it spots the difference and hands over enough context that the next person doesn’t have to start again.

Then time the human work all the way to resolution. That’s the number I’d put in front of leadership.

QA:Illustrative arithmetic, not a benchmark or observed customer result:80*3+20*40=1040 versus100*10=1000.
