Senior CSMs should own consequential customer work.

They should not become the default owner for every consequential thing a customer mentions.

A support incident needs a technical answer.

A roadmap request needs a product decision.

A commercial exception needs authorised approval.

The CSM can assemble the context, protect customer continuity and make the consequence visible.

They often cannot make the binding decision.

That is why “own the outcome” is incomplete advice.

Use four work modes instead:

Own: CS has the authority and capability to decide.

Prepare: another function decides; CS supplies customer evidence and a recommendation.

Coordinate: a time-bounded handoff needs customer continuity, an answer date and a stop rule.

Remove: the work is repetitive, automatable, duplicated or creates no enduring value.

Customer proximity creates responsibility for context.

It does not create authority over every decision.
